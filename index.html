How it works

Built for kids who learn differently

Most curricula assume a child who speaks, sits and follows instructions. This one starts from where your child is.

Interest-led

Every lesson starts from what your child already loves: a jingle, a game show, cars, water. Their interest is the way in, not a reward to earn.

Regulation first

Swings, pool, quiet time and movement sit around every learning block. A calm child learns; an overloaded one can't.

Communication everywhere

Gestures, signs, AAC, scripts, speech: every lesson includes a chance to request, choose, comment or say no.

Short and real

5–10 minute blocks for young or high-support learners. Shop trips, cooking and the car count as lessons and as evidence.

Sample lesson plan · Ages 9–11

Every lesson fits on one card

The same seven-part format across the whole curriculum, so you can run it in minutes and write your own.

Shopping with a picture list

MathematicsHASS10–15 min
Goal
Finds 3 items from a picture list and counts them into the basket
Hook
One item on the list is their favourite snack
Steps
  1. Make the picture list together at home
  2. At the shop, point to item 1 and find it together
  3. Tick it off, then the next item
  4. Count the items at the checkout
Communication
Model "found it!" and "all done!"
Adjustments
Quiet time of day, headphones, leave before overload
Evidence
Photo of the ticked list, plus H / P / I

Moderator meeting

Your evidence folder, sorted

Keep these six things in one folder or phone album, and the yearly evaluation becomes a conversation about how your child is going.

  • Your child's goals from this curriculum, with your adjustments noted
  • A simple weekly timetable
  • Help / Prompt / Independent tracking sheets
  • 2–3 dated photos or short videos per learning area each term
  • Therapy reports that show related goals
  • A one-page summary of progress since the last evaluation

Why we made this

Spectrum Home Ed started with a West Australian dad home-educating his nonverbal autistic son, who loves jingles, game shows and swings. The existing options were built for kids who talk and sit at a desk. This is the curriculum we wished we'd been handed on day one.

Questions parents ask

Is home education legal for a child with a disability?

Yes. In WA, any parent can register as a home educator with the Department of Education. Your child's program can be adjusted to their needs; the Department says programs should be flexible to individual needs and abilities.

Will this meet the education department's requirements?

Every age stage maps its goals to the eight WA Curriculum learning areas, which is what moderators check against. The final decision is always your moderator's, so use the evidence folder checklist and talk to them early about your adjustments.

When does registration start and end in WA?

From the start of the year your child turns 5½ (Pre-primary) until the end of the year they turn 17½ or 18, whichever comes first. You book your first moderator meeting within 3 months of registering, then yearly.

What about Years 11 and 12?

SCSA offers Preliminary courses for students with an intellectual disability or learning difficulty. Units are chosen to fit an individual plan. Preliminary units alone don't count toward WACE, but VET, workplace learning and endorsed programs such as ASDAN can.

We're not in WA. Does it still work?

The teaching approach works anywhere. Each state registers home educators differently (NESA in NSW, VRQA in Victoria, the Home Education Unit in Queensland, and so on), but most expect the main learning areas to be covered. Check your state's rules before registering.

My child doesn't talk. Can they still do this?

Yes. This curriculum was written for nonverbal and minimally speaking children first. Pointing, signs, a phrase board, AAC and scripts all count as communication, and as progress.
